# The CHANGEReadyX™ Assessment Method

This document describes the method behind the open-source CHANGEReadyX™
readiness assessment: what it measures, how it is scored, and — plainly — what
it cannot do.

## The premise

Transformation programmes are governed by milestones: design complete, build
complete, training complete, go-live. Milestones measure the programme's
progress. They say nothing about the organisation's readiness to absorb the
change — and it is readiness, not programme progress, that decides whether the
business case survives contact with daily work.

The CHANGEReadyX™ discipline is therefore sequenced the other way round:

1. **Readiness before milestones.** Diagnose the organisation first — before
   the investment case is finalised and before the plan hardens. A readiness
   gap found in diagnosis costs a workshop. The same gap found after go-live
   costs the business case.
2. **Role-based diagnosis.** Whole-population averages hide the failure.
   Readiness is assessed role by role and group by group: who is confident and
   committed, who is uncertain, and who is quietly planning to work around the
   new system or process. The workarounds are not a discipline problem — they
   are the most honest readiness data a programme gets.
3. **Manager enablement as the transmission layer.** People take their cues
   about a change from their own manager, not from the programme team. If
   managers are not equipped before their teams — what to say, what to model,
   how to handle resistance — no volume of programme communication compensates.
4. **Adoption-to-value measurement.** Go-live is a progress check, not a finish
   line. Adoption is behaviour: who is transacting in the new way of working,
   role by role, week by week — tracked until the change in behaviour shows up
   as the business outcome the investment promised.

## The instrument

Ten dimensions, three questions each, answered on a 1–5 evidence scale:

| Score | Anchor |
| ---: | --- |
| 1 | Not in place — no evidence |
| 2 | Emerging — informal or inconsistent |
| 3 | Defined — documented but unevenly practised |
| 4 | Established — practised and evidenced in most areas |
| 5 | Embedded — practised, evidenced and reviewed routinely |

Questions are phrased as behaviours and evidence ("Can you name…?", "Have you
measured…?"), not opinions. When a respondent cannot point to the evidence, the
honest score is low — and that is the instrument working as intended.

## Scoring

- Dimension score = mean of its three answers, rescaled: `(mean − 1) / 4 × 100`.
- Overall readiness index = mean of the ten dimension scores.
- Bands: 0–39 **Exposed** · 40–59 **Emerging** · 60–79 **Ready** ·
  80–100 **Commanding**.

Interpret the profile, not just the index. Transformation outcomes are
constrained by the weakest dimensions — typically manager enablement, adoption
visibility and conviction — which is why the report always surfaces the three
lowest dimensions as the intervention order.

## How to run an assessment well

- **Assess in a group, argue about the evidence.** The score matters less than
  the disagreement it surfaces between leaders who each believed readiness was
  someone else's job.
- **Separate the raters.** Executives, managers and end-user representatives
  score the same programme very differently. Collect the perspectives
  separately before you blend them — the gaps between perspectives are findings
  in themselves.
- **Re-assess on a cadence.** A baseline before commitment, a check before
  go-live, and an adoption review after. Readiness is a moving quantity; a
  single snapshot decays fast.

## Limitations — stated honestly

- **Self-report.** Scores reflect what respondents believe and can evidence in
  the room. They are vulnerable to optimism bias, especially in leadership-only
  groups. Triangulate with system usage data, process metrics and frontline
  interviews wherever possible.
- **Evidence-based, not validated.** The instrument is built from practitioner
  evidence and field observation across enterprise transformation programmes.
  It is **not** a validated psychometric instrument: it has not been subjected
  to formal construct-validity or reliability testing, and its scores should
  never be presented as clinical or statistical measurements.
- **A diagnostic, not a verdict.** The output is the starting point for a
  readiness conversation and an intervention plan — not a pass/fail judgement
  on a programme or its people.
- **Open-source edition.** This release contains the core question set and
  scoring. The full proprietary CHANGEReadyX™ diagnostic — including the
  complete instrument, Constraint Triage™ and the Transformation Intelligence
  Index (TII™) — is a TAPAS Advisory engagement and is not part of this
  repository.
